# Challenges

This project is yours to shape. Day 1 gets everyone set up. After that, pick
challenges from **Make it yours**, in any order, and make the sandbox into
something your team is proud to show on **Tuesday, December 1**.

---

## Day 1: first steps

**Everyone:**

1. Play with the [sample sandbox](https://emenriquez.github.io/cs1101-falling-sand/)
   for five minutes. Hover over each element to read its rules. Find out
   what happens when lava meets water, and when fire meets oil.
2. Make your own element file with **one** element in it, add it to
   `list.txt`, and get it merged (README section 3 has the steps).
   **Done when:** your element is in the palette on your team's live link,
   and your name is in the line under the title.

**The owner, too:** change `title:` in `list.txt` to your team's name for
the sandbox, and pick a `theme:`.

**The whole team:** open an issue called `Project plan`
(**Issues** → **New issue** → **Blank issue**) that answers:

- What kind of world are you building? (A volcano lab? A garden? A kitchen? An alchemy table?)
- Who's making which elements?
- Which challenges below are you picking?
- What will you show on December 1?

You can change the plan later. Edit the issue when you do.

## Make it yours

**Pick at least 3** (teams of 4: at least 4). Each one says whether it's
for one person or the whole team. Every element has to come in through a
pull request that a teammate approved.

### A. Chain reaction · *one person or a pair*

Make something happen that makes something else happen, that makes
something else happen. Lava melts ice into water, the water makes the plant
grow, the plant catches fire, the fire makes smoke…

- Draw it on paper first: arrows from each element to what it becomes.
- Use both kinds of rules: `becomes … when touching …` changes the element
  itself, and `turns … into …` changes its neighbors.

**Done when:** you can start it with one stroke of the brush and watch at
least **three different changes** happen in a row, and a teammate can make
it happen from your instructions alone.

### B. Something alive · *one person*

An element that grows, spreads or moves on its own, like the plant in
`basics.txt`. Moss that creeps across stone. A fungus that eats wood. A
swarm of fireflies that only lasts a few seconds.

- `turns empty into …` lets things grow into blank space.
- A small chance, like `(0.5%)`, makes growth slow and natural.

**Done when:** you paint a little of it, leave the sandbox alone for 30
seconds, and the world has clearly changed.

### C. Fire safety · *one person or a pair*

Make three elements: one that **burns**, one that **puts fire out**, and one
that **won't burn at all**.

**Done when:** you can build a scene where a fire spreads, gets stopped by a
wall that won't burn, and gets put out by your extinguisher. Share the link
in your `Project plan` issue.

### D. Balance it · *one person*

Pick anything that spreads: fire, a plant, goo. Too high a chance and it eats
the whole world in five seconds. Too low and it fizzles out. Find the
numbers that make it *interesting*.

- Change one number at a time. Test each try with **Test a draft**.
- Keep notes: what you tried, and what happened.

**Done when:** your pull request description lists at least three numbers
you tried and what each one did, and why you picked the final one. (That's
an experiment, and it's exactly how game designers tune real games.)

### E. Weird science · *one person*

An element that breaks the rules of the real world. Sand that falls up. A gas
so heavy it sinks. Ice that grows when it touches fire. A liquid that turns
everything it touches into gold.

**Done when:** a teammate who didn't read your file is surprised by it, and
the hover description in the palette explains exactly why it happens.

### F. Element family · *one person*

At least **four** elements that all belong together and react with each
other: a kitchen (flour, water, dough, bread, burnt toast), a volcano, a
swamp, a potion shop.

**Done when:** every element in your family has at least one rule that
mentions another element in the family, and none of them are just a
recolored copy of a basic element.

### G. The machine · *whole team*

Build a contraption in the sandbox, out of everyone's elements: a sand
hourglass that sets off a fire, a water wheel of reactions, a Rube Goldberg
machine. Then **Share this world**.

**Done when:** the link is in your `Project plan` issue, and another team
watched it run and guessed what would happen *before* it happened.

### H. Squeeze the link · *one person*

The share link is short because it counts runs of the same thing instead of
listing every cell (read `docs/HOW-IT-WORKS.md`). Make two worlds with about
the same number of filled cells, where one link is **at least five times
longer** than the other.

**Done when:** you can explain to your team, in two sentences, why one link is
so much longer. Write those two sentences in your `Project plan` issue.

### I. Conflict on purpose · *two people*

If your team hasn't hit a merge conflict yet, make one, so it's not scary
later. Two people each add a new element file and its line at the bottom of
`list.txt`, in two separate pull requests, at the same time. Merge one. The
other one now has a conflict.

**Done when:** you resolved it on GitHub (README section 6), both files are
in `list.txt`, and both people's elements are in the palette.

## Before December 1: show and tell

You'll have about **two minutes**. It's informal: no slides, just your live link.

- [ ] The live link is open and shows **✓ No problems**.
- [ ] Each person can point to one element they made and say what its rules are.
- [ ] You have one share link ready that shows off the best thing your
      sandbox does (put it in your `Project plan` issue).
- [ ] Be ready to say one thing that surprised you, or one thing that was
      harder than it looked.
